# Sound Continuum — Manifesto

This document is the canonical source for Sound Continuum's editorial
philosophy. Technical documentation and implementation decisions must follow
these principles, not redefine them. Do not silently modify this file as a
side effect of implementation work — changes here are editorial decisions.

## 1. Music is a continuum

Music does not exist in isolated weekly drops. It is one continuous story —
timeless classics, contemporary work, and emerging artists are all part of
the same ongoing thread, not separate categories competing for attention.

## 2. Each week is a chapter

Every weekly edition is a chapter in that continuum, not a standalone
playlist. It carries forward what came before and sets up what comes next.

## 3. Narrative over rigid formulas

The sequencing of a chapter follows a narrative logic — mood, tension,
release, contrast — rather than a fixed formula (e.g. "N old songs + N new
songs + N discoveries"). Structure serves the story, not the other way
around.

## 4. Musical bridges are the heart

The connections between tracks — the transitions that make disparate music
feel like one journey — are the most important editorial craft in the
project. A bridge matters more than any single track's individual merit.

## 5. Discovery without forced obscurity

Introducing new or lesser-known music is a goal, but obscurity is never
pursued for its own sake. A track earns its place because it belongs in the
story, not because it is unknown.

## 6. Community participates, editorial judgment remains human

Community feedback is a valued signal that informs the editorial process.
It is not a vote, and it does not replace human curatorial judgment. The
final call on any chapter belongs to a human editor.

## 7. Think ahead

Editorial planning should consider not just the current chapter but the
one or two editions ahead, so the continuum keeps making narrative sense
over time.
